# Ice under a dry cover: lunar south pole (Diviner) vs Kazakhstan (MODIS)

## Moon: Diviner PRP south, random sample of 80,000 of 2,880,000 surface triangles (latitudes -89.9 to -76.1)

- annual maximum temperature: median 248 K, 5th pct 117 K
- triangles with maximum temperature below 110 K: 4.6%
- model says ice stable somewhere in the column: 38.4%; at the surface (depth 0): 4.8%; only under a dry layer: 33.6%
- where ice is buried: depth median 0.08 m (10th-90th pct 0.01-0.46 m); surface there averages 93 K and peaks at 220 K

## Where the analogy breaks (measured)

- Temperature gap: the coldest Kazakh site averages 264 K; lunar ground with buried ice averages 93 K. Ice is lost by melting on Earth and by sublimation into vacuum on the Moon.
- Cover scale: metres of rock debris on Earth against a modelled 0.08 m of regolith on the Moon.
- The Earth depth uses an assumed diffusivity and ignores latent heat, snow and water flow.
- What does match: in both places ice is absent at the surface and survives below a dry layer that damps the temperature wave.
